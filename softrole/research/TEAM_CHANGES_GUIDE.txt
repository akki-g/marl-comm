 Guide 3 | Team changes and
 information-role recovery
 Can the team use the backup when its usual source
 fails?
 Question: When the usual scout loses its sensor, can the team use another capable scout—
 or has it learned a coordination pattern that works only with the original assignment?

 Key deliverable: An empirical characterization of failure recovery, separating lost physical
 capability from failure to use a surviving information source. The intervention is primarily
 training-data and evaluation design, not a new architecture.

 Recommendation: My preferred alternative to Guide 1 under your scope constraint. Keep
 three agents throughout and study sensing-role handover. Do not begin with arbitrary new
 teammates, variable populations, or hypernetworks.

 Basis: The report's third direction, p. 20, and primary literature checked on 19 September
 2026. The fixed-team design below is narrower than general ad hoc teamwork. All new
 outcomes remain hypotheses.

 1. The contribution we would try to make
 A candidate claim is: robustness to random missing messages does not necessarily
 imply robustness when useful information moves to a different teammate; varying
 information roles during training can reveal or reduce that gap.

 This is not a claim that source diversity or failure training is new. The possible contribution is
 the controlled comparison: same network, same number of agents, same surviving
 capabilities, and matched communication conditions, with a measured explanation of the
 remaining recovery failure.

 The alternative outcome is equally important: ordinary shared policies may already handle
 the handover. That would narrow the role-recovery concern for the tested setting instead of
 motivating another architecture.

 2. What prior research already covers
 ROMA learns role representations and specialized, dynamic behaviors. “Agents learn roles”
 is already an established contribution. We would not implement a competing role-learning
 network. [1]

 HyperMARL studies shared learning and agent specialization using hypernetworks,
 including problems associated with coupling IDs and observations. It is relevant background,
 not a required replacement for our shared actors. [2]

 JaxAHT provides teammate-generation and unseen-partner evaluation infrastructure. It
 matters if we later introduce independently trained replacement policies. A sensor-role
 handover among jointly trained agents is not the same claim as cooperation with unfamiliar
 teammates. [3]

 Message-Dropout establishes missing-message training as a robustness baseline. The key
 comparison here is whether that exposure transfers to a different source/role configuration.
 [4]

 A recent PL-MARL preprint also studies sparse communication and node failure in aerial
 coverage. Broad “resilient MARL under node failure” is therefore not a fresh claim. Its
 physical-inference approach is a distinct method; our proposed study isolates information-
 role recovery instead. [5]

 These are adjacent precedents, not a certification that the exact handover comparison is
 untouched. Before the main study, check their experiments and references for role
 randomization, sensor failures, and information redundancy—not only their paper titles.

 3. A task small enough to finish
 Use two potential scouts and one responder. The scouts can visit a sensing area to discover
 which of two service sites needs attention. The responder travels to the correct site. A fresh
 request is generated each round, so old information cannot solve every later request.

 Both scouts are capable of sensing. Initially one is positioned or assigned to observe, while
 the other is a potential backup. In the failure condition the usual scout loses sensing, but
 keeps movement and radio. The backup remains able to reach the sensing area and deliver
 new information.

 The task must remain physically solvable with the surviving capabilities. A scripted handover
 controller establishes this before learning experiments. Do not remove the only working
 sensor and then call the resulting impossibility poor coordination.

 Keep the team at three agents and retain fixed-width observations. This avoids the variable-
 team encoder changes described in the audit. Current PCP's homogeneous predators do not
 already implement these sensing roles; use a new task version. [R, pp. 7, 20]

 Announce the failure in the first study
 Give all compared methods the same explicit capability/failure flags, without revealing the
 request itself. This tests response to a known failure, not diagnosis of an unobserved
 failure.

 Use valid/invalid flags for missing measurements instead of letting a zero coordinate look like
 a legitimate observation. A short declared observation-history window can be used
 consistently across methods; it is not unrestricted memory.

 Separate two evaluation conditions
 Controlled handover: after switching the source, ensure the backup can obtain the next
 cue without extra travel. Keep one useful source and the same permitted packet schedule.
 This checks whether the team can use a different source under simplified geometry.

 Physical recovery: the backup must move into sensing range after failure. This adds the
 real role-reassignment/navigation problem. Its reference controller has the same remaining
 sensors and speed limits.

 Do not interpret a controlled-handover failure as purely “message-language incompatibility.”
 Changed local states, optimization, and history could also matter. Compare against explicit-
 information and movement controls before attributing an internal mechanism.

 4. Exactly what we ablate during training
 Use the existing Broadcast policy first. Cross two factors:

  Factor                        Condition A                Condition B

  Information-role              Same scout starts as the   Starting useful scout is randomized
  assignment                    useful source.             between episodes.

  Message availability          Clean delivery.            Independent packet dropout at a fixed
                                                           declared rate.


 Keep training frames, model sizes, message widths, task distributions, and opportunity to
 transmit the same within each dropout condition. In the controlled fixture, hold physical
 positions and site identities fixed while varying which scout receives the cue. Use equal-
 travel geometry to avoid giving one source an easier mission. Do not simply swap every
 agent label, position, and observation together: for a symmetric shared policy that is a
 relabeling, not a meaningful training ablation.

 Four Broadcast cells × three development seeds = 12 communicating pilot policies. Add
 LocalCapacity under fixed and randomized starting roles: two cells × three seeds = six local
 policies, giving 18 pilot policies total. No local runs need to be duplicated merely because
 their nonexistent channel has a dropout setting.

 During this first training phase, roles do not switch within an episode. Evaluation introduces
 that transition. The randomized condition therefore has experience with each scout serving
 the role, but not necessarily with recovering from a mid-episode failure.

 5. The tests that distinguish the explanations
 Evaluate each policy on intact episodes, controlled handovers, and physical sensing failures.
 Pair the request sequence and environment randomness across comparable conditions.
 Begin with a fixed midpoint handover; reserve other failure times and geometries for a later
 held-out check.

 First question: is the team still capable? The scripted controller with surviving
 capabilities should complete post-failure requests. Its score is a feasible reference, not a
 proven optimum.

 Second question: does information-role exposure matter? Compare the fixed-role and
 randomized-role Broadcast cohorts under the same dropout training condition. Report both
 their intact performance and the change caused by failure. Otherwise, a difference in basic
 task competence can be mistaken for a recovery effect.

 Third question: does packet-dropout training cover this failure? Compare its
 improvement on message outages with its improvement on sensing-role handover. The task
 loses a sensor in one case and messages in the other; do not equate those disruptions
 simply because both reduce received data.

 Fourth question: is the effect specific to one aggregator? If the pilot reveals a clear
 mechanism, repeat the decisive training contrast with existing Attention. A result only for
 Broadcast should be described as such, not as a universal property of MARL.

 The small seed counts above are for development. Before the final comparison, freeze one
 primary contrast and choose the training-seed allocation using pilot variability and a
 practical effect size. Use disjoint development/test scenarios, average episodes within each
 policy, and report every seed and failed run.

 A crucial symmetry check
 A pure relabeling of identical agents, with observations and masks permuted consistently,
 should not change a permutation-equivariant shared policy's behavior apart from relabeling.
 Broadcast pooling alone does not imply a learned dependence on sender identity.

 Run this as a null test. A real sensing failure changes capability and information; changing
 the order of array entries is not the same intervention. Do not add ID embeddings just to
 create a problem to solve.

 6. Outcomes and measurements
 Use the fraction of post-failure requests served before their deadlines as the
 primary endpoint. Show request-by-request success aligned to failure time, time to the first
 correct post-failure service, duplicate/wasted travel where relevant, and communication cost.
 Retain episodes that never recover instead of averaging recovery time over successes only.

 Report a recovery penalty for each cohort: intact success minus failure-condition success.
 The main training-ablation effect is the difference in those penalties between fixed and
 randomized roles, accompanied by the raw success values. This helps separate general skill
 from failure sensitivity.

 For mechanism evidence, log when the backup first sees the new cue, when a useful packet
 reaches the responder, and whether the responder makes the correct commitment.
 Suppressing each sender separately can identify dependence of the frozen policy, but large
 attention weights or message magnitude alone do not establish utility.

 Possible finding: randomized roles reduce handover loss even when packet dropout alone
 does not. To strengthen that conclusion, show comparable intact competence, physically
 solvable failure episodes, and repeated effects across seeds and a held-out setting.

 Contrary finding: both curricula recover equally well. A precise bounded null can still be
 useful; it is not evidence that all changing-team problems are solved. Do not continue adding
 artificial asymmetry until a failure appears.

 7. Deliverable, implementation, and novelty assessment
 The final deliverable is a fault-recovery study with a training-ablation table, a recovery curve,
 and a diagnostic separating “backup never obtained information” from “information arrived
 but the task still failed.” Release the failure-event definitions, observation contract, policies,
 and per-seed evidence.

 Reuse MAPPO, LocalCapacity, Broadcast, and optionally Attention. Add the versioned task,
 capability flags, failure scheduling, paired event evaluation, and recovery metrics. Do not
 add variable populations, new role embeddings, new losses, or unfamiliar policy
 replacements in the first paper. [R, pp. 18–20]

 Novelty potential: a focused information-role transfer result could be distinctive and
 directly relevant to resilient robot teams. A generic node-removal experiment is much
 weaker. The hardest part is causal interpretation, especially distinguishing changed
 geometry and useful-information availability from an alleged communication-protocol
 problem.

 First deliverable: the two-scout/one-responder fixture and a scripted controller that
 succeeds after a known sensor failure. Then run the controlled handover before the full
 physical-recovery study. This is my second choice because it keeps architecture work small
 while asking a concrete failure question.
